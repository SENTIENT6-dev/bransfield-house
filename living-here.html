New residents

Welcome. The Welcome Package (PDF) covers the building in one document. A few things to do in your first days:

  1. Register with the office. Fill in the Resident Registration Form. Tenants also provide a copy of the lease.
  2. Have your fobs reprogrammed. Bring the fobs you were given to the office so they carry your name and the right access, including the pool change rooms.
  3. Book the elevator for your move at least two weeks ahead. See moving and deliveries.
  4. Register your vehicle and any pets with the office.
  5. Ask for your Condo Control login. The resident portal is where notices are posted and where you submit service requests.
  6. Activate your internet and TV. Bell Fibe is included with the suite. See internet and TV.
  7. Arrange unit insurance. See insurance.

Internet and TV

Bell Fibe internet and TV are included in the monthly fees for every suite, delivered on Bell's fibre network. You do not pay Bell for the package below, but each household has to activate it.

Internet
Up to 1.5 Gbps down and 940 Mbps up, unlimited
TV
Fibe TV "Better" package with Crave

What is included

  • Fibe Internet: up to 1.5 Gbps download and up to 940 Mbps upload, unlimited usage, the Bell modem, and a Wi-Fi pod if the technician recommends one.
  • Fibe TV: the "Better" programming package, Crave with Starz, time shifting and up to 2 smart (Android) 4K PVRs.
  • Installation.

How to activate

  1. Call Bell's Preferred Building line at 1-888-988-0818. Everyone needs to call, including existing Bell customers.
  2. Tell the agent you live in a Preferred Building and give your full address with unit number and postal code.
  3. Have your Bell account number ready, or a piece of ID if you are new to Bell.
  4. Book the installation, and allow four to six hours for it.

Bell sends each household a monthly bill with the package credited in full. Anything you add, such as extra channels, more receivers or home phone, is billed to you by Bell. For billing or technical help with the service, call the same number.

Parking and visitors

Your space

  • Each suite has its own assigned space in the underground garage, included with the suite. It stays with the suite when it is sold. Register the vehicle that uses it with the office.
  • You may rent your assigned space only to another resident of the building.
  • One vehicle per space. A bicycle and a shopping cart may be kept in the space as well; nothing else is stored there.
  • Vehicles can be up to 2.08 m (6 ft 10 in) high and 20 ft long. No commercial vehicles, trailers, boats or RVs.
  • Drive at 10 km/h in the garage with your headlights on. Cars leaving the garage have the right of way.
  • No repairs and no car washing on the property.

Renting an outdoor space

The corporation does not rent out indoor spaces. If you need a second space, outdoor spaces are rented by the month, currently $100, with the rate reviewed each year in the budget. Ask the office to be added to the waiting list. Outdoor rentals are for owners who live in the building, one per suite, and the space does not stay with the suite when the owner moves out.

The office building at 401 The West Mall, at Burnhamthorpe, also rents parking to the public through Impark.

Visitors

  • Visitor parking is the three rows at the north end of the outdoor lot. There is no charge.
  • Overnight guests need a permit. The resident, not the visitor, asks for it at the security desk or the office and shows photo ID.
  • Get the permit before 1:00 am and display it on the dashboard. Vehicles without one are ticketed after 2:00 am.
  • Each suite can have permits for up to 3 nights a week and 12 nights a month. The security desk issues up to three nights in a row; for a longer stay, speak to the property manager.
  • Residents may not park in visitor parking.
  • Stopping at the front door is limited to five minutes, with the driver at the car.

Electric vehicles

There are two charging stations for residents. Download the ChargeLab app and register to use them. They are for charging only, once a day per resident: move the car back to your own space within 15 minutes of the charge ending.

Vehicles parked in someone else's space, in a fire route, or without a permit can be ticketed and towed at the owner's expense. Repeat offenders can be towed without notice and lose visitor parking privileges.

Rules, sections 8 and 9, and the Welcome Package

Pets

  • Up to two pets per suite: dogs under 50 lb or no taller than 24 inches at the shoulder, cats, small caged animals, small birds or an aquarium.
  • Register every pet with the office using the Pet Registration Form.
  • In common areas, pets are carried or kept on a leash no longer than six feet. Dogs and cats wear a collar with ID.
  • Dogs pass through common areas only to enter and leave. They may be walked on-leash on the green space behind the building, away from the playground, and off-leash in the dog off-leash area.
  • Pick up after your pet, every time.
  • Ask before stepping into an elevator that already has people in it.
  • Visitors may not bring pets into the building.

Garbage and recycling

Chute hours
7:00 am to 10:00 pm
Recycling
Dumpster behind the building, not the chute
Furniture
Locked bulk area: ask to have it opened
  • Garbage: tie it in plastic bags and put it down the chute on your floor. Nothing loose, and nothing left on the chute room floor.
  • Too big for the chute: carry it down and put it directly in the garbage dumpster behind the building.
  • Recycling: carry it down to the recycling dumpster behind the building. Flatten cardboard boxes.
  • No plastic bags in the recycling dumpster. They tangle in the sorting machinery, which causes damage and extra cost for the building. Empty your recycling out of the bag.
  • Furniture and other very large items: these go in the fenced bulk area behind the building. It is kept locked, so ask the superintendent or the office to open it.
  • Tires, paint and other hazardous items cannot be left in the bulk area. The City of Toronto's Waste Wizard tells you where each item goes. Lithium batteries go to a household hazardous waste drop-off.
  • Renovation debris: you or your contractor must take it away. None of it may be left on the property.
  • Never put anything burning down the chute.

Rules, section 5, and the Welcome Package

Heating and cooling

  • Each suite has its own electric ceiling-coil heating. Older thermostats can take one to three hours to reach the temperature you set.
  • Avoid setting the temperature very high or opening windows to adjust it. Suites are designed to stay comfortable without window ventilation.
  • Heat your own suite. Warmth is not meant to carry through floors and ceilings, so do not rely on your neighbours' heating.
  • In-suite heating and air conditioning are the owner's responsibility. For repairs or adjustments, contact the office, which can help or coordinate with your electrician.

Repairs

Inside your suite
The owner's to repair or replace
Outside your suite
The corporation's to repair or replace
  • Yours: appliances, vents, plumbing fixtures, light fixtures and the like inside the suite.
  • The corporation's: suite doors and locks, outside windows, hallway walls and the other common areas.
  • When in doubt, ask the property manager before arranging any work.
  • For a problem in a common area, submit a service request on Condo Control or tell the office.

Chargebacks

If the office arranges a repair in your suite that is the owner's responsibility, you are sent a copy of the invoice with a chargeback letter. You pay the invoice amount only, with no added fee. Pay it by the date in the letter: unpaid chargebacks, like unpaid maintenance fees, can lead to a lien on the unit.

Noise and quiet hours

  • Washers, dryers and dishwashers are not run between 11:00 pm and 7:00 am, or before 9:00 am on Saturdays, Sundays and statutory holidays.
  • At any hour, noise that carries into other suites or the hallways and disturbs neighbours is not allowed. That includes music and instruments.
  • Renovation work is limited to weekdays, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm. See renovations.

For a noise problem after office hours, call the security desk. For a police matter that is not an emergency, such as a noise disturbance that does not stop, the Toronto Police non-emergency line is 416-808-2222, or *877 from a mobile phone.

Rules 3.5, 3.6 and 3.26

Smoking

  • Smoking and vaping are not allowed anywhere on the common elements. That includes hallways, stairwells, the garage, the grounds, and balconies and patios.
  • Smoking is allowed only inside a suite, and smoke or odour must not travel into other suites or the hallways.
  • Never throw cigarette butts or ashes from a balcony or window.

Rules 3.15 and 4.8 to 4.10

Balconies and hallways

  • Balconies are for seasonal furniture and planters with live plants, secured against the wind. No storage, no carpet and no drying laundry.
  • Nothing hangs over or sits on railings or outside window sills, and nothing is shaken or thrown over the side.
  • Keep water from running onto the balconies below.
  • Hallways stay clear: no doormats, shoes, strollers or other items outside suite doors.
  • No signs or notices that can be seen from outside the suite without the Board's consent.

Rules 3.10 to 3.25

Renovations

Anything beyond decorating needs the Board's written approval before work starts. Allow 10 to 14 business days for review, longer for major work.

Work hours
Monday to Friday, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm
Not permitted
Weekends and statutory holidays

What to submit

  • Unit Renovation Agreement
  • Unit Renovation Plan, with the scope of work and schedule
  • Contractor Information for each contractor, with an insurance certificate naming YCC 86 and the owner (at least $2 million liability) and proof of WSIB coverage
  • Flooring Renovation Agreement if you are changing floors
  • Any drawings and permits that apply

Things to know

  • New flooring needs a sound barrier underneath with an Impact Insulation Class (IIC) rating of at least 72. Send the manufacturer's specification sheet with your request. See the underlay guide.
  • Structural and load-bearing walls, and any pipes, wiring or ducts that serve other suites, cannot be altered.
  • Electrical work is done by a licensed electrical contractor with an Electrical Safety Authority permit.
  • Before work starts, the common areas near your suite are inspected and recorded. Any damage during the job is repaired at the owner's cost.
  • Do not give fobs, common-area keys or garage access to contractors. Book the service elevator for materials and debris.

Renovation agreement and Rules 3.2 and 3.17

Renting out your unit

  • Leases are in writing and for at least six months.
  • No short-term rentals of any kind, including Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com.
  • A suite is rented as a whole to one household. No renting of single rooms and no sublets.
  • Within 10 days of signing a lease, give the office a copy of it or a completed Form 5, Summary of Lease or Renewal, along with the tenant's registration form.
  • Give your tenant a copy of the Declaration, By-laws and Rules.
  • Tell the office in writing within 10 days when a tenancy ends, and make sure fobs, keys and garage remotes come back.
  • No more than two people per bedroom.
  • Owners are responsible for any damage their tenants cause to the common elements.

Rules, section 7

Bikes and e-bikes

  • No riding bikes, scooters, skateboards or rollerblades anywhere on the common elements, including the garage and ramps.
  • Bicycles are kept in the bike room, on the outdoor rack or in your own parking space. See bike room and rack.
  • E-bikes and other electric mobility devices are not allowed in the interior common areas.
  • Only e-bikes and batteries with a Canadian certification mark (cUL, CSA or cETL) may be used or stored in the building. Use the manufacturer's charger, never charge unattended, and keep batteries away from anything flammable.

Rules 4.4, 4.5, 8.23 and section 10

Keys, fobs and security

  • Do not prop open or unlock building doors, and do not buzz in anyone you do not know.
  • New owners and tenants: bring your fobs to the office to be reprogrammed with your name and access.
  • Fobs, keys and garage remotes are ordered through the office and paid for in advance. Order forms are available there.
  • A lost or broken fob is not replaced free of charge. Tell the office right away if you lose one, so it can be deactivated.
  • Suite keys are high-security keys that only the building's supplier can copy.
  • Keep a working smoke alarm in your suite, and report pests or leaks to the office right away.

Rules 3.8, 3.9, 4.1 and 4.2, and the Welcome Package

Insurance

The corporation insures the building. It does not insure what is yours. Owners and tenants are strongly urged to carry their own condominium unit policy that covers:

  • Your belongings, in the suite and in a locker or vehicle
  • Improvements and upgrades made to the suite
  • Personal liability
  • Living expenses if you have to move out after a loss
  • The corporation's insurance deductible, if it is charged back to your unit

By-law No. 7 (Standard Unit and Insurance Deductible) sets out what the corporation insures and what falls to the owner. Insurers often ask for it when you arrange your policy: download By-law No. 7 (PDF).

Rules, section 12, and By-law No. 7

Board and management

  • YCC 86 is governed by a five-member Board of Directors. A term is usually three years.
  • The annual general meeting is held once a year, before June 30. Every owner is encouraged to take part.
  • The Board sets the budget each year. The fiscal year ends on December 31, and the budget is mailed to owners around the first week of December.
  • ICON Property Management runs the building day to day, and the property manager reports to the Board.
  • Owners can pay fees and update their contact details through Condo Control. Debit and credit card payments carry a service fee charged by the payment processor, not by the corporation or management.
